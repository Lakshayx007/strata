# SIMULATED CRM-style opportunity and usage dataset

> **All data in this section is SIMULATED.** It is generated by `analysis/crm_sim/generator.py`
> from parameters in `data/crm_sim/params.yaml`. It is not real CRM data. Tables are prefixed
> `sim_` to prevent confusion.

## Why it is simulated

Real CRM data is proprietary and confidential. This simulation demonstrates the kind of
operational analysis a PM would do with access to CRM, usage and delivery data. The loss-reason
mix is **calibrated** to phase-1 stated-reason shares (cost 20/63, operational simplicity 11/63,
lock-in 8/63, performance 8/63, right-sizing 7/63, ecosystem fit 4/63, governance 2/63, other
3/63), so the simulated pipeline reflects the reason distribution found in public discussion.
This is a calibration choice, not observed CRM data.

## Generator logic

- **Seed:** 42 (deterministic; re-running produces the same data)
- **sim_accounts:** 1,500 accounts with industry, region, tier, deployment mode, ARR band.
  Region weights: NA 40%, EU 30%, APAC 22%, ROW 8%.
- **sim_opportunities:** 4,000 opportunities over 8 quarters (Q1 2024 – Q4 2025). Amounts
  drawn from a log-normal distribution (~$100K median). Win rate: 35%. Lost deals get a
  loss_reason drawn from the phase-1 calibrated weights.
- **sim_stage_history:** stage entry/exit dates for each opportunity through the pipeline stages.
- **sim_usage_monthly:** per account per service (engineering, warehouse, streaming, AI),
  monthly consumption units and active users with a growth trend.
- **sim_renewals:** per account: start ARR, expansion, contraction, churn flag. Churn rate: 8%,
  expansion rate: 30%, contraction rate: 12%.
- **sim_releases:** PM team × sprint delivery data: planned vs delivered points, on-time flag,
  planned vs actual release dates. 4 teams × 8 quarters × 6 sprints = 192 rows.

## Queries and results

All SQL is in `analysis/crm_sim/queries/`, one file per question. Results are in
`analysis/export/pipeline.json`.

### 1. Win rate by competitor (`win_rate.sql`)
In this simulation, win rate is uniform across competitors (~35%) because the generator
assigns outcomes independently of the competitor field. A real dataset would show variation.

### 2. Loss-reason mix by competitor (`loss_reasons.sql`)
In this simulation, loss reasons follow the same distribution for every competitor (the
phase-1 calibrated weights). Cost is the top loss reason at ~32% of losses.

### 3. Stage-to-stage conversion (`stage_conversion.sql`)
In this simulation, the Qualification and Technical Evaluation stages have the most entries.
Median days in stage varies by the deal cycle length.

### 4. Regional adoption by service (`regional_adoption.sql`)
In this simulation, North America has the most accounts (40%). Warehouse has the highest
average consumption, streaming the lowest.

### 5. Churn by usage decile (`churn_cohort.sql`)
In this simulation, churn is approximately uniform across usage deciles (~8%) because the
generator assigns churn independently of usage. In reality, low-usage accounts churn more.

### 6. Expansion drivers (`expansion_drivers.sql`)
In this simulation, expansion rates are similar across tiers and industries (~30%) because
the generator is tier-independent. Large enterprises would typically expand more in reality.

### 7. Delivery velocity (`delivery_velocity.sql`)
In this simulation, all PM teams deliver at about 72% on-time rate with similar velocity.

## Five insights (in this simulation)

1. **In this simulation,** cost is the top loss reason at ~32% of lost deals, matching the
   phase-1 finding that cost is the most common stated reason for platform choice.
2. **In this simulation,** the overall win rate is ~35%, with no significant variation by
   competitor — a limitation of the uniform random assignment.
3. **In this simulation,** churn is ~8% and approximately uniform across usage deciles,
   suggesting the model needs usage-dependent churn to be more realistic.
4. **In this simulation,** warehouse consumption is ~1.2x engineering consumption, reflecting
   the workload share assumptions from the market model.
5. **In this simulation,** PM teams deliver at ~72% on-time rate with an average slip of
   ~4 days for late releases.
